# Wordplay mechanisms

Use this reference when the brief calls for wordplay. Mechanisms can overlap;
choose the label that explains the construction. Wordplay is one naming approach,
not a requirement for every concept.

| Mechanism | What changes | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homophonic or near-homophonic substitution | Replace a word with one that sounds identical or similar. | Pronunciation and whether the audience can recover the source. |
| Sound or syllable substitution | Replace a small part while retaining recognizable material. | Changed sounds, preserved syllables, stress, and rhythm. |
| Blend or portmanteau | Combine parts of words, sometimes at an overlap. | A natural join, legibility, and spoken form. |
| Phrase or name parody | Retain a familiar structure while replacing a component. | Recognition and the intended association. |
| Graphic or numerical wordplay | Letters, symbols, or numbers carry part of the meaning. | Spoken usability as well as visual recognition. |
| Semantic double meaning | One form supports multiple relevant interpretations. | Whether both meanings fit the concept and audience. |
| Repetition or prosodic parody | Retain rhythm, repetition, stress, or intonation. | Whether the pattern survives speech, not just spelling. |

## Sound checks

Compare source and candidate aloud. Identify changed sounds or syllables,
preserved material, and stress. Distinguish onset, vowel, coda, and whole-syllable
changes when useful. Use approximate respelling unless precise pronunciation is
supported. Recognition depends on audience and pronunciation; a small sound
change is an option, not a quality guarantee.

## Source banks

Choose languages, reference territories, and vocabulary from the brief. Relevant
sources can include ordinary speech, objects, actions, technical terms, nature,
places, practices, stories, or media. Expand through meanings, associations,
verbs, sounds, and structures rather than repeatedly inserting one keyword.

Verify unfamiliar terms and cultural claims. Mark supplied but unverified words.
Recognition within one community does not establish recognition elsewhere.
Keep source banks specific to the task; prior examples do not define the subject
or cultural default for another request.

## Reusable naming prompt

Include concept, purpose, audience and languages, tone, naming approaches,
relevant source territories, constraints, rejected directions, output size, and
evaluation fields. Include examples only with the traits they illustrate. For
pun candidates, ask for the source and transformation. Keep collision screening
as a separate stage.

Show selection criteria without invented audience testing or unseen candidate
pool counts.
