# Creative naming

Find names whose sound, meaning, and visual possibilities fit the audience. Clever spelling is not automatically a spoken joke or a viable brand.

## Establish the brief

Read examples, prior batches, concept, audience, languages, selected identity, and rejected directions. Use constraints already supplied. Distinguish brand names, in-game jokes, character names, and feature labels; they tolerate different levels of obscurity and reference dependence.

Choose analysis, a reusable naming prompt, generation, or shortlist research according to the request. Do not replace a naming-prompt request with candidates.

## Build from sources and sounds

Use [WORDPLAY.md](WORDPLAY.md) to distinguish mechanisms and record transformations. Build an audience-appropriate source bank: phrases, people, media, places, objects, practices, and vocabulary. Pair it with a semantic bank for the subject.

- Compare pronunciation, rhythm, syllables, and stress, not spelling alone. Prefer recoverable changes when recognition matters.
- Preserve references accurately. Verify unfamiliar names, quotes, meanings, and cultural claims from primary sources or knowledgeable speakers; label supplied but unverified dialect words.
- Do not flatten Tagalog, Hiligaynon, Cebuano, and other languages into interchangeable vocabulary. Record intended language and audience.
- Use approximate respelling when helpful. Give IPA only when supported; do not fabricate precise dialect or celebrity pronunciations.
- Explore ordinary words, double meanings, concise compounds, and invented words as well as puns. Do not force mascots into every name.

## Generate and select

Explore mechanisms, then reject weak recognition, awkward pronunciation, repeated jokes, forced compounds, and the wrong product implications. Match batch size and fields to the request. Candidate counts, category quotas, and numerical scores are optional brief choices.

For finalists, show name, source/construction, sound or meaning change, fit, and tradeoff. Test ordinary speech appropriate to the product: inviting a friend, naming a character, or sharing a creation. These are design checks, not evidence of audience preference.

For another round, avoid earlier names and spelling variants. Keep a compact prior-candidate record in established naming notes when useful. Strong names can suggest a family: app, mascot, features, items, or character roles.

## Separate judgment from availability

Rank creative quality independently from collision risk. For a recommended commercial shortlist or requested availability check, research current use through relevant official sites, registries, stores, and domains. State what was checked. Search absence is not trademark clearance or domain availability. A borrowed reference can be funny while unsuitable for a primary brand.

Use `art-direction` when the selected name's visual identity is requested. A batch does not authorize renaming the project or replacing assets.
