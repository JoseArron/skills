# Wordplay mechanisms

Mechanisms can overlap. Choose the label explaining the change rather than decorating a table with terminology.

| Mechanism | What changes | Supplied example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Near-homophonic substitution | Replace a word with a similar-sounding word. Exact homophones sound alike; near matches do not. | Hev Abi → Web Abi: two consonants change in the approximate spoken form. |
| Localized sound or syllable substitution | Change a small part while preserving the source. | Vice Ganda → Vice Gamba: retain `Vice` and the surname's two-syllable frame. |
| Blend or portmanteau | Combine pieces of source words. Overlap can ease the join. | Turon + tarantula → Turontula. |
| Phrase or name parody | Retain a familiar structure while replacing a component. | Biag ni Lam-ang → Biag ni Damang. |
| Graphic or numerical wordplay | Writing carries part of the joke; check the spoken form too. | IV of Spades → VIII of Legs. |
| Semantic double meaning | One form has two relevant interpretations without sound changes. | A modding name built around `patch` or `house rules`. |
| Repetition or prosodic parody | Retain rhythm, repetition, or intonation. | Weather Weather Lang → Webber Webber Lang. |

These analyze the user's supplied constructions; they do not independently verify every reference or pronunciation.

## Sound checks

Compare source and candidate aloud. Identify changed sounds/syllables, preserved material, and stress. Distinguish onset, vowel, coda, and whole-syllable changes when useful. Avoid calling every blend a homophone.

Recognition depends on audience and pronunciation. One-sound changes are useful options, not quality guarantees. Larger substitutions can work when the source survives.

## Source banks

For Philippine audiences, draw from the languages and communities in the brief. Media, performers, food, landmarks, expressions, heritage, and ordinary speech provide structures. Verify unfamiliar words. Recognition by one audience does not establish nationwide recognition.

For spider themes, territories include local animal names, webs, weaving, silk, legs, fangs, movement, catching, hanging, and spinning. User-supplied regional species names remain supplied vocabulary until language and meaning are checked. Expand through verbs, sounds, and double meanings rather than repeatedly inserting `web`.

## Reusable naming prompt

Include concept, audience/languages, tone, source territories, examples, mechanisms, pronunciation constraints, rejected directions, output size, and evaluation fields. Ask for source and transformation for pun candidates. Keep collision screening a separate stage.

Do not claim audience testing or a particular unseen candidate-pool size. Show evidence or describe selection criteria without invented process counts.
